# Mask experiment: solid fill vs blur over the same clues (D-174)

Generated by `benchmark/bench/score-maskexp.mjs` (2026-11-26). Counts and rounded distances only.

**Question.** Blur-based redaction tools hide a sign by blurring it. Strangers' Eyes paints a solid block. Does the blur leave the attacker more to work with?

**Method.** The held-out benchmark photos where the attacker cited at least one removable clue (and never refused): 15 photos, 15 scored. For each, the clue boxes it cited (padded 15%) were hidden two ways on the same redacted copy: the app's **solid fill**, and a **Gaussian blur** strong enough that a person can't read the text (sigma = 15% of the box's shorter side, at least 8 px at 2048 px). Faces are solid in both. `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` attacked each version 3× (a photo is "within 5 km" if 2 of its answered runs are), interleaved (solid, blur, solid, blur, …). Pre-registered in the private protocol before the run.
